Many gardens look good for the first month and then slowly decline. The usual reason is not bad plants. It is that nobody was responsible for the small, regular jobs.

A maintenance plan, often called an AMC, fixes that by putting those jobs on a schedule. The useful ones also put them in writing.

## What a typical visit can cover

The exact list depends on your plan and your space, but a routine visit often includes:

- Checking the soil moisture and adjusting watering
- Pruning and removing dead or damaged leaves and flowers
- Loosening compacted soil and topping up compost where needed
- Looking for pests and disease early, when they are easy to treat
- Checking drainage so water does not collect around roots
- Turning or moving pots so plants grow evenly toward the light

## What the written scope should say

Before you sign, ask for these points in writing. Our own plans define them for every customer.

1. **Visit frequency.** How often someone comes, and on which fixed days.
2. **Included work.** The jobs that are part of every visit.
3. **Material exclusions.** What costs extra, such as new soil, fertiliser or planters.
4. **Response times.** How quickly someone returns if something goes wrong between visits.
5. **Replacement rules.** What happens when a plant dies, and who pays for the new one.

<Callout title="A useful test">
If two people could read your plan and disagree about what is included, the scope is not clear enough.
</Callout>

## Proof after every visit

You should not have to be home to know the work was done. A short summary and photographs after each visit let you see the condition of the space, spot trends and ask questions early.

## Renewal without surprises

A good provider contacts you before the plan ends, not after. We start renewal conversations 30 to 45 days before expiry so you have time to compare options and adjust the plan to the season.

## Choosing a plan

Compact balconies usually need routine care. Larger terraces and fuller gardens benefit from pruning and soil conditioning as well. Offices, hotels and societies often need defined service levels and reporting. The GardenCare plans are built around those three situations.
