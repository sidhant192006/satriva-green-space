Both spaces can become a green room. They are different projects, though, and treating a terrace like a large balcony is a common and expensive mistake.

## A balcony is a short project

Balconies are small, usually close to the kitchen or living room and easy to water. A first project can be finished in a few days: planters, a handful of well-chosen plants and a simple watering routine.

Start here if you want to see results quickly, are new to plants or want to test how much care you enjoy giving.

## A terrace is a design project

A terrace is larger and more exposed. Before any plants arrive, it is worth thinking through four things.

### Weight

Soil, water and planters are heavy, and wet soil is heavier than dry soil. Check with your builder, architect or society whether the slab can carry the load you have in mind, and spread weight toward walls and columns rather than the middle of a span.

### Waterproofing

Do not place soil directly on a bare roof surface. Keep the waterproofing layer in good condition, and use planters or trays that protect it. If the roof already leaks, repair that first.

### Water

A terrace with many pots needs a plan for watering. Irrigation lines and timers save a lot of effort, and they are much easier to lay out before the planters go in.

### Sun and wind

Terraces get more sun and more wind than balconies. Plant choices and pot sizes should reflect that, and tall plants may need support or a sheltered corner.

<Callout title="Society and rental rules">
In apartment buildings, terraces are often shared or restricted. Confirm permission and any rules about weight, drainage and visible changes before you spend money.
</Callout>

## How to compare quotes

Ask each provider for good, better and best options and for a breakdown of scope, labour, materials and travel. A price quoted only per square foot hides the difference between a thin layer of plants and a well-planned garden.

## A sensible path

1. Book a site visit so someone can check the space in person.
2. Choose the option that fits your budget and the way you will use the space.
3. Start with the area you will see and use most, then extend.
4. Add a care plan so the first season does not undo the work.
