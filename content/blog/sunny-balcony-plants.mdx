A balcony that faces south or west can feel like a hot plate by April. Plants that thrive in a nursery often struggle there. The fix is not more watering. It is choosing plants that suit the light you have.

## Start by counting the sun

Stand on the balcony at three points in the day, morning, noon and late afternoon, and note where direct sunlight falls. Six or more hours of direct sun counts as full sun. Three to five hours is partial sun. Anything less is shade, even if the balcony feels bright.

<Callout title="Good to know">
Glass, grills and the building opposite change how much sun reaches the pots. Count the light on the pots themselves, not the light in the room.
</Callout>

## Plants that tend to cope with full sun

These are commonly grown in Indian balconies and handle strong light once they are established.

- **Bougainvillea.** Flowers best in strong sun and a little neglect. Prune after each flush of flowers.
- **Portulaca and vinca.** Low, colourful and tolerant of dry spells, which suits hot railings and ledges.
- **Hibiscus and jasmine.** Both flower well in bright light, although they drink more water than the plants above.
- **Aloe vera and jade plant.** Succulents store water in their leaves, so a missed day or two does little harm.
- **Tulsi, rosemary and lemongrass.** Useful herbs that like sun. Keep mint in a separate pot with some afternoon shade.

## Plants for partial shade

If the balcony gets only a few hours of direct sun, snake plant, money plant, peace lily and most ferns are more forgiving. Keep them out of the hottest afternoon sun, which scorches leaves.

## Pots matter as much as plants

- **Choose larger pots than you think you need.** Small pots heat up and dry out quickly.
- **Use drainage holes and a saucer.** Water that cannot leave the pot rots roots. Empty saucers after rain.
- **Terracotta dries faster than glazed ceramic or plastic.** That helps plants that dislike wet roots and hurts thirsty ones.
- **Group plants by water need.** One pot of succulents among thirsty ferns will be overwatered or the ferns will be underwatered.

## Wind and heat on higher floors

Upper floors are windier, and wind dries soil faster than sun does. Place tall plants against a wall or in a corner, and consider a light shade net over the hottest afternoon hours in summer. Check the soil with a finger before watering. If the top two to three centimetres are dry, it is usually time.
